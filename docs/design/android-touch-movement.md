# Android movement and action gestures

[design, #2436] Android defaults to an analog movement stick and fire-button drag look. The Controls > Touch settings retain the legacy directional stick and stationary fire button as explicit alternatives. Desktop has no touch resource and keeps its existing behavior. Run lock and look-area double-tap VATS are separately opt-in.

[observed: player.rs] Existing controller axes preserve direction but velocity/swim_velocity normalize every nonzero vector. Touch analog therefore needs an explicit speed share defaulting to one for all legacy inputs. Touch samples use a radial dead zone and clamped radius, obey current directional bindings, input capture, controller/touch selection, platform quarantine, furniture/view ownership and script locks. Central ControlInput action/script readers include admitted touch directions, while player arbitration uses an explicit digital-only directional query. IsControlPressed receives an independently supplied, default-empty virtual-control set; IsKeyPressed/key counts and raw ButtonInput remain physical. The new transient ScriptInput field is not serialized, and no persisted format changes. Explicit script movement flags and test-aid movement reset the speed share to their own native pace. Native walk/run speeds and the existing small-stick walking threshold remain authoritative; there is no sprint or new AP behavior. Physical directional controls take priority over a simultaneous touch sample.

[design] Fire contact ownership remains the original Use binding while its owning finger can also send look motion. Moving off the fire circle does not release the shot. A single look owner prevents multiple fingers from adding competing motion. End/cancel/device/menu/editor/focus transitions release only touch ownership.

[design] Run lock is a movement-thumb upward release past the displayed auto-move target, or a deliberate tap on that target. The target yields to user-positioned action buttons and hides when that space is unavailable. It pulses the user's existing AutoMove binding and displays actual PlayerControl.auto_move. It does not maintain a second movement toggle or alter AlwaysRun. A fresh admitted movement push cancels native AutoMove as the original Forward/Back press does. Script/input locks can reject the pulse normally.

[design] Optional VATS is two short, stationary taps on unclaimed look space. Look drags, multiple contacts, cancellation, viewport changes, consumed controller-return touches, modal forms and loading covers cancel the sequence. The result pulses the user's existing Vats binding; the actual VATS rules choose whether to draw, open or refuse. No target selection or AP rules are duplicated.

[qualification] Meaningful App tests must observe native MoveIntent/velocity, PlayerControl trigger/AutoMove and VATS admission through real bindings, plus modal/lifecycle/hardware ownership controls. Source checks and Android typechecks do not prove device feel or physical rendering.
